Fireworks Galaxy

Face-on spiral galaxy (NGC 6946) · 25 million light-years

How big is Fireworks Galaxy?

Fireworks Galaxy is about 86,000 light-years across.

See Fireworks Galaxy in 3D, at true scale

The Fireworks Galaxy, NGC 6946, is a barred spiral seen face-on, on the border of Cygnus and Cepheus. William Herschel found it in 1798. It is busy making stars, and its many supernovae earned it its name.

Good to know

  • The James Webb telescope found over 5,000 Earths' worth of dust around supernova SN 2004et, evidence that dust can survive the blast.
  • In 2009 a star of about 25 Suns here brightened, then vanished from visible light without a proper explosion. It may have collapsed straight into a black hole.
  • Its red knots are clouds of hydrogen lit by newborn stars.
