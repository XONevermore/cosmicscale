Enceladus

Moon of Saturn · 1.4 billion km from the Sun

How big is Enceladus?

Enceladus is about 504 km across.

See Enceladus in 3D, at true scale

Enceladus hides a global ocean of salty water beneath an icy shell. More than a hundred geysers at its south pole jet that water hundreds of kilometres into space, where it feeds one of Saturn's rings. The Cassini spacecraft flew straight through the plumes and tasted them.

At a glance

Diameter504 km
Mass1.08 × 10²⁰ kg
Diameter compared with Earth0.040 × Earth
OrbitsSaturn, every 1.4 days
Ocean depthAbout 10 km, under 5–25 km of ice
SurfaceAbout −200 °C
Discovered1789, William Herschel

Good to know

  • Its fresh ice reflects about 80% of the sunlight that hits it, making it the most reflective body in the Solar System.
  • Cassini found salts, silica and organic molecules in the plume — signs of warm water meeting rock on the ocean floor.
  • The spray forms Saturn's faint E ring, which the moon flies through.
