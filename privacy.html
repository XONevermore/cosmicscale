Cosmic Scale — Privacy Policy

Effective 30 October 2026

Cosmic Scale ("the app") is published by Sardor Urdushev. This policy explains what information the app handles.

What the app itself collects

Nothing about you. The app has no accounts and no sign-in, and its only server is the small one behind the space chat, described below. What it stores stays on your device: the language you chose, when your Beyond pack and Ultra unlocks expire, which simulations you have unlocked, and whether Cosmic Scale Pro is on. It never leaves your phone. The only things the app sends anywhere are the crash and freeze reports, the usage statistics and the daily notification described next, the questions you ask in the space chat, and what the advertising, purchase and photo services below receive.

Crash and freeze reports

If the app crashes or stops responding, it can send a report to Google's Firebase Crashlytics so the fault can be found and fixed. The report holds technical details of the failure (which part of the app's code was running and which screen was open), the app version, your device's model and Android version, its free memory and storage, and a random installation identifier that Firebase creates. It holds no name, email address or location, nothing you typed, and it is not used for advertising. Firebase processes it for us under Google's terms: see Privacy and Security in Firebase.

Usage statistics

To see which parts of the app people use, the app can send anonymous usage statistics to Google Analytics for Firebase. It records events such as which screens and simulations are opened, how far the welcome tour gets, whether a video was watched to unlock something, whether Pro was bought, and which language is set. It sends no name, email address, location, advertising ID or anything you typed, and Analytics here does not use the advertising ID or ad personalisation. Google Analytics adds the technical details every app report has: the app version, your device's model, Android version and language, your country and city (worked out from your IP address, which Google does not keep), and a random installation identifier. Google processes this for us under its terms: see Privacy and Security in Firebase.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the UK or Switzerland, nothing is collected unless you agree in the consent form that appears when the app starts. Everywhere, you can turn it off in Settings → Share usage statistics, which also deletes what Google Analytics holds for your installation. The statistics are not collected by test builds.

Your approximate location (optional)

The Sky screen can tell you which eclipses you can see from where you are. Only if you tap "Use my location" does the app ask Android for your approximate location (the "approximate location" permission, never your precise one). The app rounds it to about 10 km, keeps it only on your phone, and uses it only to work out what is visible from there. It is never sent to us, to Google Analytics or to anyone else, and the sky is worked out on your phone. You can remove it at any time with "Forget my place" in the Sky screen, or take the permission away in your phone's settings. The Sky alerts are set on your phone with Android's alarm service and need no account or server.

Daily notification

The app can show one notification a day. It uses Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging: the app joins a notification topic, which makes Firebase create a random installation identifier and a message token for your device. Nothing else about you is sent, and the topic holds no personal information. You can turn the notification off in Settings (the app then leaves the topic), or in your phone's notification settings. Firebase processes this for us under Google's terms: see Privacy and Security in Firebase.

Advertising

The app is free and shows ads through Google AdMob (or, in Russia, the Yandex Advertising Network: see below), unless Cosmic Scale Pro is on: then no ad SDK is started at all. To serve and measure ads, the Google Mobile Ads SDK may collect and process information such as your device's advertising ID, IP address (from which approximate location can be inferred), device and app information, and interactions with ads. Google's use of this information is described in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services and the Google Privacy Policy.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the UK or Switzerland, the app asks for your consent through Google's User Messaging Platform before personalised ads are shown. You can change your choice at any time from About → Privacy choices in the app.

Google serves no ads in Russia, so a phone there shows ads from the Yandex Advertising Network instead, through the Yandex Mobile Ads SDK. The app decides this on the phone, from the country of your mobile network or SIM card (or, without either, your phone's language setting); it does not use your location, and phones elsewhere never load the Yandex SDK. To serve and measure ads, the Yandex Mobile Ads SDK and AppMetrica, Yandex's analytics library that comes with it, may collect and process your device's advertising ID, IP address, device and app information (such as the phone model, Android version, screen and battery level), crash and diagnostic reports about the ad SDK, and interactions with ads. They do not collect your location. Yandex's use of this information is described in the Yandex Privacy Policy.

You can also reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of ad personalisation, in your device's Google settings.

Space chat

The space chat (the ✨ button) answers questions about space. When you send a question, the app sends it, together with up to three earlier questions and answers from the same conversation, the app's language and a random identifier made only for the chat, to our small service on Cloudflare Workers. That service passes the questions and answers to Anthropic's Claude, which writes the reply. Like any web request, Cloudflare receives your device's IP address.

The service keeps no copy of your questions or the answers. It keeps only a count of questions per day for the chat identifier and for your IP address, so that nobody can go over the daily limit; the counts start again each day. Anthropic processes the questions and answers to write the reply, under its commercial terms, which do not allow it to train its models on them: see Anthropic's Privacy Policy. The app does not store your conversation, and nothing is sent until you send a question. Please don't type personal information into the chat.

If the service can't be reached, a phone with Google's built-in AI (Gemini Nano) may answer on the phone itself instead; that answer is worked out on your device and nothing is sent.

Purchases

Cosmic Scale Pro is sold through Google Play, as a monthly or yearly subscription or a one-time purchase. Google Play handles the payment; the app never sees your payment details. It only asks Google Play which Pro purchases your Google account owns, to turn Pro on, and keeps that answer on your device. Google's handling of purchases is described in the Google Privacy Policy. You can manage or cancel a subscription in Google Play at any time.

The HUAWEI AppGallery version

The version of the app from HUAWEI AppGallery runs on phones without Google's services, so three things above work differently there. Ads come from the Yandex Advertising Network in every country (never Google AdMob), as described above for Russia; where the consent form has to ask (the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland), only non-personalised Yandex ads are requested. The daily notification uses HUAWEI Push Kit instead of Firebase Cloud Messaging: the app joins a notification topic, which makes HUAWEI Mobile Services create a push token for your device; nothing else about you is sent. Cosmic Scale Pro is sold through HUAWEI In-App Purchases: HUAWEI handles the payment, the app never sees your payment details, and it only asks which Pro purchases your HUAWEI ID owns. You can manage or cancel a subscription in your HUAWEI ID settings. HUAWEI's handling of this data is described in the HUAWEI Privacy Statement.

Photos from space agencies

When you open an object's details, the app loads real photographs directly from the servers of NASA (images-assets.nasa.gov), ESA/Hubble (esahubble.org), ESA/Webb (esawebb.org), ESO (eso.org) and NSF NOIRLab (noirlab.edu). Like any website, those servers receive your device's IP address and a standard request for the image. The app sends them nothing else. Their own privacy policies apply to those requests.

Sounds from space missions

When you tap Listen, the app streams that sound or sonification from NASA (nasa.gov), the Chandra X-ray Center (chandra.harvard.edu) or the University of Iowa (uiowa.edu). As with photos, those servers receive your IP address and the request, nothing else. Nothing is streamed until you tap Listen. The tour music is generated on your device.

Shorts Studio (our YouTube upload tool)

Shorts Studio is a separate internal tool, not part of the app. It runs only on our own computer and is used only with our own YouTube channel, UNIVERSESCALE. It uses YouTube API Services: by using it you agree to the YouTube Terms of Service, and Google's handling of the data is described in the Google Privacy Policy.

With the channel owner's permission (Google sign-in), it uploads and schedules our own videos, sets their title, description, tags and privacy, lists our channel's uploads with their view counts, and reads our channel's YouTube Analytics (views and average percentage watched) to tell us which videos did not do well. It does not read, collect or store data about viewers, commenters or any other channel. It keeps on our computer only the sign-in token, the IDs and schedule times of our videos, and for at most 30 days their titles, view counts and percentage watched, and it sends our own alert messages to us through Telegram. Nothing is shared with anyone else or used for advertising. Stored records are deleted when they are no longer needed, and at the latest 30 days after access is revoked.

Access can be revoked at any time on the Google security settings page (Third-party apps & services → Shorts Studio).

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from them.
