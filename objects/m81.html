Messier 81

Grand-design spiral (Bode's Galaxy) · 11.8 million light-years

How big is Messier 81?

Messier 81 is about 96,000 light-years across.

See Messier 81 in 3D, at true scale

Messier 81, Bode's Galaxy, is a grand-design spiral with a large yellow bulge and two clean arms. Johann Elert Bode found it in 1774. It is the biggest member of a group of galaxies that includes Messier 82.

Good to know

  • About 200 million years ago it swung close to Messier 82, and their gravity pulled out long streams of hydrogen that are still there.
  • The newest measurement, from the James Webb telescope, puts its central black hole at about 48 million Suns, ten times the Milky Way's.
  • Its bulge is noticeably larger than the Milky Way's.
