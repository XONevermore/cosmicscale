S5 0014+81

Black hole of a blazar · 11.6 billion light-years

How big is S5 0014+81?

S5 0014+81 is about 4.4 × 10¹⁰ km across.

See S5 0014+81 in 3D, at true scale

S5 0014+81 is a blazar: a quasar whose jet points almost straight at Earth. The black hole behind it was built up in under 2 billion years after the Big Bang. No telescope can show it as more than a point, so this view is an illustration, with the disk close to face-on as we see it.

Good to know

  • Its jet points within a few degrees of Earth, which is why it looks so outrageously bright.
  • It was once called the heaviest black hole known, at 40 billion Suns; better infrared data cut that by about five times.
  • In 1983 it was named the most luminous quasar known.
