Black hole sizes compared: from Gaia BH1 to TON 618

The size of a black hole is the size of its event horizon, which grows in step with its mass. A black hole of a few Suns is a city wide; the giants in the cores of galaxies would swallow the Solar System.

Black holes

Phoenix A*

Phoenix A* is the black hole at the heart of the Phoenix Cluster, and current estimates put it near 100 billion Suns — possibly the most massive known.

TON 618

TON 618 is an ultramassive black hole powering a quasar so bright that we can see it from over ten billion light-years away.

Holmberg 15A*

Holmberg 15A is the brightest galaxy of its cluster, and its centre is strangely empty of stars — the signature of a huge black hole that scattered them away during past galaxy mergers.

NGC 4889

NGC 4889 is a giant elliptical galaxy in the Coma Cluster, hosting one of the largest black holes ever weighed: about 21 billion Suns.

S5 0014+81

S5 0014+81 is a blazar: a quasar whose jet points almost straight at Earth.

M87*

M87* was the first black hole ever photographed.

NGC 1277's black hole

This black hole sits in NGC 1277, a small galaxy in the Perseus Cluster.

H1821+643

H1821+643 is a quasar: a black hole swallowing gas so fast that it outshines its galaxy.

Cygnus A's black hole

This black hole powers Cygnus A, a giant galaxy in a cluster and one of the strongest radio sources in the sky.

M84's black hole

This black hole is at the centre of Messier 84, a giant galaxy in the Virgo Cluster.

Sombrero's black hole

This black hole is at the centre of the Sombrero Galaxy, inside its huge glowing bulge.

Andromeda's black hole

This black hole sits at the heart of the Andromeda Galaxy, our big neighbour.

Sagittarius A*

Sagittarius A* is the supermassive black hole at the centre of the Milky Way, 26,700 light-years away.

M32's black hole

This black hole is at the centre of Messier 32, a small dense galaxy beside Andromeda.

Gaia BH3

Gaia BH3 is a black hole of 33 Suns found in 2024 from the wobble of a star orbiting it every 11.6 years.

Cygnus X-1

Cygnus X-1 is a black hole of about 21 Suns locked in a 5-day orbit with a blue supergiant star.

LMC X-1

LMC X-1 is a black hole in the Large Magellanic Cloud, a small galaxy orbiting ours.

Gaia BH1

Gaia BH1 is the closest known black hole to Earth, found not by any glow but by watching a Sun-like star swing around something invisible.

V404 Cygni

V404 Cygni is a black hole of about 9 Suns pulling gas off a close orange star.

XTE J1650-500

XTE J1650-500 is a black hole in Ara, found when it flared in X-rays in 2001.
