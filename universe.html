How big is the universe?

The scale of space, from asteroids to the observable universe

The observable universe is about 93 billion light-years across: that is the region whose light has had time to reach us in the 13.8 billion years since the Big Bang. That is about 6.9 × 10¹⁹ times wider than Earth, and 9.3 × 10⁵ times wider than our galaxy.

If Earth were a grain of sand

  • Earth: a grain of sand 1 mm wide.
  • The Sun: a ball 11 cm wide, 11.7 m away.
  • The nearest star, Proxima Centauri: 3,160 km away.
  • The Milky Way: 74 million km across.
  • The observable universe: 69 trillion km across, about 460,000 times the real distance from Earth to the Sun.

Questions

How big is the universe?

The observable universe is about 93 billion light-years across. It is wider than 13.8 billion light-years because space has kept expanding while the light was on its way. The whole universe beyond what we can see may be far larger, or infinite; nobody knows.

How big is the Milky Way?

About 100,000 light-years across, with a few hundred billion stars. Light takes about 100,000 years to cross it.

How many Earths fit in the Sun?

About 1.3 million Earths would fit inside the Sun by volume.

What is the biggest thing in the universe?

The largest structure known is the Hercules–Corona Borealis Great Wall, a wall of galaxies about 10 billion light-years long. Its size is still debated, as is whether walls this large fit the standard picture of the cosmos.
