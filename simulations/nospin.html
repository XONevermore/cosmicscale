What if Earth stopped spinning?

Earth's turn slowed to a halt: what a sudden stop would do, why the seas would pour towards the poles, and one sunrise a year

A what-if: physics allows it on paper, but no one has ever seen one. Everything shown follows the theory as it stands.

Watch it in 3D

Step by step

A sudden stop

The only gentle stop is a slow one. So from here on, the spin winds down over many years, slowly enough for winds and currents to settle, as Esri's Witold Fraczek assumed when he mapped this in 2010. What the slow stop does is quieter, but it remakes the map of the world.

A slow stop

The rock cannot follow so easily. Earth's bulge of solid rock stays where it is, but the oceans are free to flow, and they run downhill towards the poles. On the map, old coasts by the equator rise out of the sea while northern and southern lands drown.

One sunrise a year

With no spin, Earth's trip round the Sun makes the only day: the Sun would rise in the west, cross the sky over six months, and set in the east.

Earth would still go round the Sun, so the Sun would slowly creep across the sky as Earth moved along its orbit, the other way from today: rising in the west and setting in the east. Each place would get about six months of daylight and six months of night, like the poles today but everywhere.

The weather would change completely. Today's spin turns winds sideways, which is what makes hurricanes whirl; without it, air would flow more straight from the hot day side to the cold night side. Climate models of slowly turning planets (Yang and others, 2014) find thick clouds building up under the high Sun, which reflect much of its light and keep the day side cooler than you might expect.

The rock gives way

Over thousands of years the rock flows too. The bulge sinks, Earth turns into a ball, and the seas spread back towards the equator.

As the rock settled, the sea would settle with it, and the oceans would return to something like today's coasts, because the continents float on the mantle much as they do now. How long that would take is uncertain, probably tens of thousands of years or more. The spin, though, would never come back.
