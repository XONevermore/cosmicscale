Homunculus Nebula

Two lobes thrown off by Eta Carinae · 7,500 light-years

How big is Homunculus Nebula?

Homunculus Nebula is about 0.70 light-years across.

See Homunculus Nebula in 3D, at true scale

The Homunculus is the pair of thin-walled, nearly round lobes around Eta Carinae, thrown off in the Great Eruption of the 1840s, when the star briefly became the second-brightest in the night sky. The lobes meet at a tight waist with a ragged skirt of debris around it. The mass shown is a lower limit, because it depends on how much dust there is.

Good to know

  • At 650 km/s, its gas would cross the Earth in about 20 seconds.
  • Hubble's measurements date the gas to about 1847, after the famous peak of 1843.
  • Three quarters of the mass went out through the poles: the blast itself was aimed, not pinched by a ring.
