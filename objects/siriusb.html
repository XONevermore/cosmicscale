Sirius B

White dwarf · 8.6 light-years

How big is Sirius B?

Sirius B is about 11,690 km across.

See Sirius B in 3D, at true scale

Sirius B is the burnt-out core of a star that was once more massive than Sirius itself. It has about the mass of the Sun packed into a body the size of Earth, and it no longer produces energy — it is simply cooling, and will keep cooling for billions of years.

At a glance

Diameter11,690 km
Mass2.03 × 10³⁰ kg
Diameter compared with Earth0.917 × Earth
SizeSlightly smaller than Earth
SurfaceAbout 25,000 °C
Found1862

Good to know

  • Its existence was predicted in 1844 from a wobble in Sirius's motion, 18 years before anyone saw it.
  • One teaspoon of it would weigh about five tonnes.
  • It is 10,000 times fainter than Sirius A, which is why it is so hard to see beside it.
