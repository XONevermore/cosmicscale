Halley's Comet

Periodic comet · Returns every 75–79 years

How big is Halley's Comet?

Halley's Comet is about 11.0 km across.

See Halley's Comet in 3D, at true scale

Halley's Comet is the most famous comet of all: the first known to return on a schedule, and the only bright one that does so within a human lifetime. Its dark, peanut-shaped nucleus was photographed up close by ESA's Giotto probe in 1986.

At a glance

Diameter11.0 km
Mass2.20 × 10¹⁴ kg
Diameter compared with Earth0.001 × Earth
NucleusAbout 15 × 8 km
Orbit75–79 years
Last seen1986
Next return2061

Good to know

  • Edmond Halley predicted its return in 1758; it came back on time, 16 years after he died.
  • Its nucleus is one of the darkest objects in the Solar System, reflecting only about 4% of light.
  • Dust it has shed makes two meteor showers every year: the Eta Aquariids and the Orionids.
