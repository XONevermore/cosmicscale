Antennae Galaxies

Two colliding spiral galaxies · 72 million light-years

How big is Antennae Galaxies?

Antennae Galaxies is about 410,000 light-years across.

See Antennae Galaxies in 3D, at true scale

The Antennae, NGC 4038 and NGC 4039, are two spiral galaxies caught in the middle of merging. Their tails were thrown out some 200 to 300 million years ago, and the collision is now setting off huge bursts of star birth. From tail tip to tail tip they span about 410,000 light-years.

Good to know

  • Only about 1 in 10 of the huge new star clusters here will last even 10 million years, but about a hundred of the biggest should become globular clusters.
  • Five supernovae have been seen here in about a century, and one of them, SN 2007sr, gave the distance now most used.
  • One measurement put it as near as 45 million light-years; the supernova's light puts it at about 72 million.
